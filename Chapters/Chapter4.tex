\section{System Context, Architecture, and Interfaces \applicability{Development, Hybrid, Game; Research Software Prototype}}
The system boundary contains the Flutter web/Android client, the ASP.NET Core
marketplace API, the PostgreSQL/PostGIS database, Redis, SignalR, and the
asynchronous Python/FastAPI AI service. Customers use the client for lot
discovery, communication, negotiation, and reservation. Admins manage users,
categories, thresholds, flagged listings, and AI overrides. AWS S3 stores listing
photographs. The AI service returns classification, defect, condition,
consistency, and price signals to the decision engine.

\section{Research Methodology and Experimental Design \applicability{Research, Hybrid}}
\label{sec:research-methodology}
\subsection{Study or Experimental Design}
The research part of this Hybrid project is a quantitative evaluation of existing
models and of the rules that combine them. It answers the two research questions of
Chapter~1. RQ-01 is answered offline. Each model (M-01, M-02, M-05) is run on data it
never saw during training or tuning, and its results are compared with a simple
baseline, so that a result can be judged as better or worse than doing almost
nothing. RQ-02 is answered from the working system. Automated decisions on screened lots
are compared with the Admin's decisions, and Listing Assistant answers are labelled
against the lot data. Interviews and a questionnaire supply the price data and the
requirements evidence, and they are not themselves a research question.

\subsection{Population, Sample, Participants, or Dataset}
\textbf{Public datasets.} The defect detectors are trained only on the public datasets
in \tref{tab:defect-classes}, using each dataset's own training and validation splits.
CLIP is used without training. Each dataset's licence is recorded before use (ER-05,
RSK-04).

\textbf{Team photo set.} The team photographs real surplus lots in Lahore as a seller
would: 3--5 photos per lot, taken with ordinary Android phones, at dealer yards and
construction sites, with the owner's permission. The target is 30 lots per material,
or 120 lots in total (target, to be agreed with the supervisor). A lot is included when
it is one of the four materials and is photographed in normal daylight or site light.
Photos showing people's faces, vehicle number plates, or house numbers are retaken or
excluded. Two team members label each lot's material and condition grade and draw
boxes for the defect classes in \tref{tab:defect-classes}. They settle disagreements
by discussion. The set is split by source (Section~\ref{sec:leakage-controls}) into a
tuning split of about two-thirds and a locked test split of about one-third. The tuning
split is used to set thresholds and is never used to train the detectors, because the
proposal plans a validation set rather than a training set.

\textbf{Interviews and questionnaire.} The team interviews 5--10 Lahore surplus and
scrap dealers, chosen because they trade the four materials, and asks each for 5--10
recent real sales: material, quantity, condition, age, area, and price. These give the
condition and age factors of the baseline formula and the real deals for M-05. The team
also interviews 4--6 contractors or suppliers and runs a short questionnaire on search,
negotiation, and trust features. Every participant gives informed consent (ER-01), and
notes contain no names (ER-02).

\textbf{Price data.} New-material reference prices come from published Pakistani
sources~\citep{iconssteelrate,toolsfluentcost2026,thenewscosts2026,zameenprices,graanaprices}
and are recorded with source and date (FR-86). Real surplus prices come from the
interview deals and from the optional final sale prices of Sold lots (FR-83).

\textbf{Decision and assistant data.} The decision data are the lots screened during
testing and the pilot (MS-08), with the Admin's recorded decisions (DIR-06). The
assistant question set holds about 100 questions written by the team over about 20
test lots. About half can be answered from the lot data, for example ``what is the
price per kg?''. The rest cannot, for example ``is delivery included?''.

\subsection{Analysis and Evaluation Protocol}
\textbf{Baselines.} Each RQ-01 result is compared with a simple baseline. For material,
the baseline always predicts the most frequent material in the tuning split. For
defects, it is the prototype's general building-surface model~\citep{buildingsurfacedefect}.
For price, it is the depreciation formula of M-05.

\textbf{Procedure.} Models, thresholds, the CLIP prompts, the condition-phrase list,
and the assistant instructions are frozen and versioned first. Then each model is run
once on the locked test split, and the results are reported with the model versions
(DIR-03). Because the samples are small, every proportion (VAR-01, VAR-04, VAR-05,
VAR-06) is reported as a count with a 95\% Wilson confidence
interval~\citep{wilson1927interval}, and every result is also given per material. A
result that misses its target, for example an XGBoost model that does not beat the
baseline, is reported as it is (Chapter~5).

\textbf{Decision audit.} Auto-published lots never reach the Admin, so the system alone
cannot show their errors. A random 10\% of them is therefore drawn with a recorded
random seed and reviewed offline by the Admin on an audit form, without the AI's
outcome shown. This is a research procedure, not an application feature.

\textbf{Assistant labelling.} The question set is fixed before the evaluation run.
Two team members independently label each answer as supported or not supported, and
each unanswerable question as correctly answered with the fallback or not. They see
the lot data but not the model's own \texttt{supported} value. Their agreement is
reported with Cohen's kappa~\citep{cohen1960kappa}, and disagreements are settled by
discussion before VAR-06 is computed.

\textbf{Leakage controls.}\label{sec:leakage-controls}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Locked test photos are never used to train or tune any model, threshold,
        prompt, or phrase list. They are opened only for the final evaluation run.
  \item The team photo set is split by source. All photos of one lot, and all lots
        photographed at the same site on the same day, go to the same split, so
        near-duplicate photos cannot appear in both splits. A perceptual-hash check
        across the splits finds any that remain.
  \item Tuning data are kept separate from reporting data. The 0.60 confidence
        threshold, the 0.40 detection threshold, the quality thresholds, the CLIP
        prompts, and the phrase list are set on the tuning split only. The public
        validation splits are used only to select detector weights.
  \item For M-05, interview deals are split by dealer, formula-built rows are never
        used for testing, and FR-83 sale prices are used only for testing.
  \item For the assistant, the question set is written and frozen separately from the
        questions used while writing the instructions, and labellers do not see the
        model's own \texttt{supported} value.
\end{itemize}

Create Lot, Resubmit Lot After Edits, and Relist Expired Lot each include Screen Lot,
because every new, edited, or relisted lot must pass AI screening before it can be
published. Each extend relationship marks an optional goal that starts inside its base use
case. Reserve Lot, Ask Listing Assistant, Chat With Seller, and Report Content extend View
Lot Details, because they begin from a lot's page and only when the Customer chooses them.
Chat With Seller also extends Ask Listing Assistant, because the assistant's fallback reply
directs the Customer to the seller, and Report Content also extends Chat With Seller, because
a message can be reported while chatting. Save Search extends Search Lots, and Reset Password
extends Sign In, where a user who has forgotten a password starts it. Resubmit Lot After Edits, Remove Lot, and Relist Expired Lot
extend Manage My Lots, where the Contractor starts them. Override AI Decision extends Review
Flagged Lot because an Admin overrides the AI only in some reviews. Register and Sign In
are separate goals: a user registers once but signs in for every session, and the Admin's
fixed account signs in without registering. Sign In depends on Register only as a
precondition, an existing verified account, so no relationship is drawn between them. In
the same way, being signed in is a precondition of the other use cases rather than an
included use case, because it is not a step of any user goal.

and M-03). Each photo is first checked for blur and darkness. A blurry or dark photo
is recorded and not graded (FR-43). A photo that passes is run through the YOLO
detector of the lot's classified material (FR-44), and detections below a confidence
of 0.40 are discarded. The grade decision tests Major first, so its three guards
cover every case: any defect of a major type, 3 or more defects, or one defect
covering more than 10\% of the photo gives Major; no defect gives Good; and 1 or 2
small defects of a minor type give Minor (FR-45). After the last photo, a lot with
any blurry or dark photo, or whose detection failed, is graded Not Assessed. AD-01 then
sends the first case to Changes Requested and treats the second as AI Unavailable. A
lot whose photo grades include both Good and Major is graded Mixed, because a few
damaged items may be hidden among good ones; every other lot takes its worst photo
grade, never an average (FR-46). Mixed lots always go to review (FR-27). All numeric
thresholds are provisional, are tuned on the team's tuning photos, and are never
tuned on the test set (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm-design}).

\subsection{State-Machine Model \applicability{State-dependent systems and games}}
The lot is the central stateful entity, and its reservation decides whether a lot can be
sold. \fref{fig:st-01} shows the lot lifecycle (ST-01) and \fref{fig:st-02} the
reservation lifecycle (ST-02). Both use the locked status names, and each transition is
labelled \emph{event [guard] / action}.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_4_4_3_st_lot.pdf}
\caption{Lot lifecycle state-machine diagram}
\label{fig:st-01}
\end{figure}

A new lot starts as Draft and enters Screening when it has 3--5 photos and a pickup point
inside an active zone. A blurry or dark photo returns it to Changes Requested without an
Admin decision (FR-47). Otherwise, a lot with no flags is published automatically, and a
lot with any flag, including the AI being unavailable, waits in Pending Review for the
Admin. Request Edits also leads to Changes Requested, from which the Contractor resubmits
to Screening; a lot left there for 14 days expires (FR-14). Editing a Published lot sends
it back to Screening (FR-13), so no change reaches buyers unchecked. A Published lot
becomes Reserved only through the guarded reservation in \fref{fig:st-02}, returns to
Published if that reservation is cancelled or ends in No-Show, and becomes Sold when the
Contractor marks it sold. A lot expires 30 days after publication and can be relisted
through Screening (FR-15). Rejected and Sold are final (FR-85).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{fig_4_4_3_st_reservation.pdf}
\caption{Reservation lifecycle state-machine diagram}
\label{fig:st-02}
\end{figure}

The guard on reserve, that no other Active reservation exists for the lot, is what keeps a
lot from being reserved by two buyers at once; it is enforced by a version-checked update
inside a database transaction (UC-03, SD-03, NFR-21). The Contractor ends an Active
reservation by marking it sold or, only after the 48-hour deadline, No-Show; either user
can cancel it. If no outcome is recorded within 24 hours after the deadline, the Scheduler
cancels it without penalty (UC-34), and suspending either user also cancels it (FR-19).
When a reservation ends without a sale, the lot returns to Published, or to Expired if its
30 days have passed.

After a failed attempt the worker waits a few seconds, with jitter, and retries once;
a 4xx error is not retried. The Decision Engine chooses Auto-Publish or Send to Review,
and every Mixed-condition lot goes to review. The worker saves the results, the
decision, and the status in one database transaction. If the retry fails, the lot
receives the grade Not Assessed with no price suggestion and moves to Pending Review.
After a restart, the worker resumes any lots still in Screening.

\subsection{Class or Domain Model}
The domain model is shown in two views of one model so that each stays readable at page
width. \fref{fig:class-marketplace} covers accounts, lots, and trading;

\begin{figure}[H]

\includegraphics[width=\textwidth,height=0.82\textheight,keepaspectratio]{fig_4_5_1_class_marketplace.pdf}
\caption{Marketplace class diagram}
\label{fig:class-marketplace}
\end{figure}

One User account holds both profiles and switches between them; a ContractorApplication
must be approved before the user can sell. A Lot belongs to one Zone and one Category,
whose Unit fixes the quantity unit, and is composed of 3--5 LotPhotos. A Lot has many
Reservations over time but at most one Active at once, and each buyer may hold at most
three Active reservations; these constraints are enforced in the database
(\sref{sec:data-design}). A completed Reservation can receive up to two Ratings, one from
each side. ChatThreads belong to a Lot and are composed of Messages. A Report refers to a
lot or a message, and its reporter, like a message sender, is recorded as a User
reference to keep the diagram readable.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth,height=0.82\textheight,keepaspectratio]{fig_4_5_1_class_screening.pdf}
\caption{AI screening and review class diagram}
\label{fig:class-screening}

Each screening of a lot creates a new AIAssessment, so a re-screened lot keeps its full
history for evaluation (DIR-03). An AIAssessment is composed of one PhotoAssessment per
photo, which records the photo's quality, material, and grade, and each PhotoAssessment
holds its DetectedDefects. The assessment stores the signals and the Decision Engine's
outcome with every flag reason raised, and the model versions used (FR-53, FR-58). Each
ReviewDecision records the Admin's action and the AI outcome it confirmed or overturned,
which feeds the Admin-agreement measures of the research evaluation. ThresholdConfig and
ReferencePrice keep each change with the Admin who made it. The enumerations use exactly
the locked values, so the same words appear in the state machines, the database, the API,
and the screens.

The screening path runs once per lot. The background worker sends the lot data and
pre-signed photo URLs to the AI Service (AI-01 in Appendix~B.3). There, M-01 to M-05 run
in order, and the AI Service returns signals only. The Decision Engine (M-06) runs in the
API (DD-24) and turns those signals into flag reasons and an outcome. The Listing
Assistant is a separate path. A Customer's question goes through the REST API, which
applies the rate limit and builds the lot context, to M-07, which calls the external LLM
provider (AI-02 in Appendix~B.3). The AI Service loads all screening models once at
start-up and screens one lot at a time. This keeps it within the 4 GB of memory of its
CPU-only server (ACD-05): about 0.6 GB for CLIP ViT-B/32, 0.3 GB for the four YOLO nano
models, and about 1 GB for the runtime, or about 2--2.5 GB in total. This is an estimate,
to be measured in MS-04. If the models do not fit, they are exported to ONNX or OpenVINO
(RSK-12). Training runs on Google Colab GPUs and is not part of the deployed system.

\begin{lstlisting}[basicstyle=\footnotesize\ttfamily,columns=flexible,keepspaces=true,frame=single,
  caption={Pseudocode of the condition engine (M-03)},label=lst:m-03]
function grade_lot(photos, material):
    for p in photos:
        p.quality = quality_check(p.image)  # ok, blurry, or dark
    if any(p.quality != "ok" for p in photos):
        return "Not Assessed"  # API sets Changes Requested
    for p in photos:
        d = detect(p.image, material)
        d = [x for x in d if x.confidence >= 0.40]
        if any(x.type == "major" for x in d) or len(d) >= 3 \
                or any(x.box_area > 0.10 for x in d):
            p.grade = "Major"
        elif len(d) == 0:
            p.grade = "Good"
        else:  # 1 or 2 small minor-type defects
            p.grade = "Minor"
    grades = {p.grade for p in photos}
    if "Good" in grades and "Major" in grades:
        return "Mixed"
    return worst(grades)  # order: Good < Minor < Major
\end{lstlisting}

Name and purpose & M-05 XGBoost price model. Estimates a fair surplus price range per unit, which supports a price check rather than setting the price. It treats price as a function of the lot's attributes (hedonic pricing~\citep{rosen1974hedonic}) and learns that function with XGBoost~\citep{chen2016xgboost}. Splitting and metrics use scikit-learn~\citep{pedregosa2011sklearn} (FR-50). \\
Inputs and outputs & Inputs: \texttt{sellerMaterial} and the M-01 material, \texttt{quantity} and \texttt{unit}, the grade from M-03, \texttt{ageBand}, \texttt{location} (mapped to its area in the zone), and \texttt{referencePricePerUnit}. The reference price is the new-material price per unit from the Admin's table, with its source and date (FR-86, DIR-07), taken from published sources~\citep{iconssteelrate,toolsfluentcost2026,thenewscosts2026,zameenprices,graanaprices}. Outputs: \texttt{priceEstimate} with \texttt{low}, \texttt{mid}, and \texttt{high} per unit in PKR. No estimate is produced for a Not Assessed lot (FR-28). \\
Processing steps & 1.~Baseline formula: price per unit = reference price $\times$ condition factor (grade) $\times$ age factor (age band). The starting factors are team assumptions: condition factor Good 0.70, Minor 0.55, Mixed 0.45, and Major 0.35; age factor unused 1.00, under 1 year 0.95, 1 to 3 years 0.85, and over 3 years 0.70. The dealer interviews replace them with the median ratio of surplus price to new price for each grade and age band, and every change is recorded with a new formula version.\newline
2.~Training table: (a) rows built from the baseline formula over combinations of material, grade, age band, area, and quantity band, which teach the model the formula's shape; and (b) real surplus deals reported in the dealer interviews (5--10 dealers, 5--10 recent sales each), weighted more heavily than formula rows.\newline
3.~The target is the ratio of the surplus price to the reference price, so the model stays valid when new-material prices change. The mid estimate is the predicted ratio multiplied by the reference price.\newline
4.~\texttt{low} and \texttt{high} are the mid estimate minus and plus the mean absolute error for that material, measured by cross-validation on real deals.\newline
5.~The Decision Engine raises Price Above Estimate when the asking price per unit exceeds \texttt{high} $\times$ (1 + margin), with the Admin's margin initially 25\% (FR-51, FR-52). \\
Assumptions and limits & Surplus prices are not published because the market is informal, so most training rows come from the formula, and the model can learn little beyond it. Only the real deals carry market evidence. The model is therefore tested only on real prices: held-out interview deals, split by dealer so that no dealer's deals appear in both training and test, and the final sale prices recorded under FR-83. It is never tested on formula rows. Its MAE and RMSE are compared with the baseline formula (NFR-05). If XGBoost does not beat the baseline, the result is reported as it is, and the API uses the baseline formula for the estimate instead, subject to the supervisor's agreement. Other limits: the real sample is small (about 25--100 deals), the model covers one zone (Lahore), and reference prices must be kept current by the Admin. \\

The design supports the quality goals as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Usability:} Create Lot is one screen with four numbered sections (NFR-65), a live listing preview, and a short explanation of what happens after submitting. All three roles share the same field, button, status-chip, and grade patterns.
\item \textbf{Accessibility:} Text contrast and scaling follow WCAG 2.2 Level AA~\citep{wcag22} (NFR-60, NFR-62). Grades, statuses, and flags are never shown by colour alone (NFR-64). Touch targets and screen-reader labels follow NFR-61 and NFR-63.
\item \textbf{Responsive layout:} The wireframes show the desktop layout; screens adapt to widths from 320 to 1920 logical pixels, and on a phone the panels stack in one column (NFR-68).
\item \textbf{Error prevention:} Controls stay disabled until their input is valid (FR-61), each lot row offers only the actions its status allows, and photos are compressed on the device for weak mobile data (FR-62, NFR-67).
\item \textbf{Feedback:} Each screen has a visible state for loading, AI pending, flagged, rejected, and error (FR-72), every tap gets a visible response (NFR-66), messages say how to fix a problem (FR-73), and notifications report screening results and decisions (FR-76).
\item \textbf{Privacy:} Buyers see only a lot's area until they reserve (FR-75), and the seller's address and verification note are visible only to the Admin (PR-04, PR-06).
\item \textbf{Consistency:} The grade icons, status chips, and flag names in the wireframes appear unchanged on every screen and in every role.
\end{itemize}

Accepted trade-off: a cost per question and a dependency on an outside provider, covered by the 20-questions-per-hour limit and the fallback reply (FR-30, FR-56). \\
DD-35 & Audit a random 10\% of auto-published lots offline, as a research procedure (Section~\ref{sec:research-methodology}). & No audit; send every lot to the Admin. & No Admin ever sees an auto-published lot, so without an audit its error rate is unknown. Reviewing every lot would remove the benefit of screening. Accepted limitation: the audit takes team time, and with few lots 10\% gives a wide Wilson interval. It is not an app feature. \\
DD-36 & Train one YOLOv8n defect detector for each material (M-02). & One detector for all materials; larger YOLO models. & Each material has different defect types (Table~\ref{tab:defect-classes}), and the nano size fits the 4~GB CPU-only server with the other models. Accepted trade-off: four models to train, version, and load, and the AGPL-3.0 licence question (P-27). \\
DD-37 & Send a lot with any blurry or dark photo to Changes Requested without an Admin decision, and name the photos to replace (FR-47). & Grade the photos anyway; send the lot to the Admin. & A grade from an unusable photo is unreliable, and asking the seller to retake it saves Admin time. Accepted trade-off: a seller with a poor camera may need several attempts, and the blur and darkness thresholds must be tuned on team photos. \\
DD-38 & Split the team photos by lot and by source, lock the test split, and run the frozen pipeline on it once (Section~\ref{sec:leakage-controls}). & A random split by photo; repeated cross-validation on all photos. & Photos of the same lot or site look alike, so a photo-level split lets the test set leak into tuning and overstates the results. Accepted trade-off: fewer photos remain for tuning, and a result found after the single run cannot be improved and retested on the same split. \\
DD-39 & Give the Listing Assistant no tools and require the structured reply \texttt{\{answer, supported, fieldsUsed\}} (M-07, FR-55). & A free-text reply; let the model query the database. & The API can check which fields the answer used and show the fallback when it is not supported, and a model with no tools cannot act on injected instructions (SEC-10). Accepted limitation: the assistant cannot answer anything outside the lot's data. \\
