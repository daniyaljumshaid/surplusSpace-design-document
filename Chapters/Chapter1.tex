\section{Executive Summary}
surplusSpace is an AI-gated marketplace for surplus construction materials. It
addresses the lack of a trustworthy channel through which sellers and buyers can
exchange surplus bricks, steel, tiles/marble, and wood. A seller creates a
lot with its quantity, description, price, and 3--5 representative photographs.
Before publication, the system classifies the material, detects selected visible
defects, derives a condition grade, checks description--image consistency, and
estimates a fair surplus price against a new-material reference price. A Decision
Engine automatically publishes listings that pass the checks and routes uncertain
or risky listings to an Admin, who has final authority to override the AI.
Customers and Contractors use a switchable account to search by location, price,
and category, negotiate through real-time chat, and reserve lots. An AI Listing
Assistant answers questions using only the listing's own data. The project is
limited to four material categories and evaluates classification, defect
detection, price estimation, and agreement between automated decisions and Admin
judgement. The deliverables are Android and web apps, the AI Service, and an evaluation
report, giving buyers screened listings and the Admin a smaller review workload.

\section{Background and Motivation}
Construction projects can finish with surplus bricks, steel, tiles/marble, and wood,
while construction costs in Pakistan keep rising~\citep{thenewscosts2026}, so reusable
material has value to buyers on a budget. In Lahore such material is sold today through
general classifieds such as OLX Pakistan and Facebook Marketplace~\citep{olxpakistan,facebookmarketplace},
which document no check of a listing's condition, description, or price before it goes
live. Zarea serves businesses and suppliers~\citep{zareaabout}, and Rheaply serves
organisations reusing items~\citep{rheaplyfeatures}; neither grades condition from
photos (Chapter~\ref{chap:literature}). Sellers with leftover stock and buyers looking for
cheaper material are the users affected. The project therefore combines a surplus-material
marketplace with AI screening and a human Admin, limited to four material categories so
that it can be built and evaluated within the six-month schedule.

\section{Problem Statement}
Sellers listing surplus construction material have no automated way to check,
before a listing reaches buyers, that its material, condition, description, and
price agree. Existing marketplaces~\citep{olxpakistan,facebookmarketplace} rely on
manual, after-the-fact moderation, which does not scale and leaves buyers exposed
to misrepresented lots. A marketplace is therefore needed that checks each listing
before publication while keeping a human as the final authority.

\section{Proposed Approach}
The project will develop a marketplace with Admin, Customer, and Contractor
roles. Its AI pipeline uses CLIP zero-shot classification, material-specific YOLO
defect detection, a condition engine, description--image consistency checks, and
XGBoost price estimation. A Decision Engine auto-publishes clean listings and
sends uncertain or Mixed-condition listings to an Admin review queue. The core
platform provides lot-based listings, location and category search, real-time
chat, negotiation, ratings, saved-search alerts, reservation, and pickup-status
tracking.

The research part evaluates how well the AI performs. For RQ-01, the classification,
defect detection, and price models are tested offline on a locked set of team-collected
photos and on real sale prices from dealer interviews, each against a simple baseline.
For RQ-02, automated publish-or-review decisions are compared with Admin decisions, and
Listing Assistant answers are checked against each lot's data.
Section~\ref{sec:research-methodology} gives the full method.
