\section{Literature Synthesis \applicability{Research, Hybrid; other projects where applicable}}
The project's AI pipeline rests on four themes in the literature. Each theme below
ends with the limitation that shapes the design.

\subsection{Zero-Shot Material Classification}
Conventional image classifiers need labelled examples of every class. CLIP avoids
this requirement: it is trained on 400 million image and text pairs to predict which
caption goes with which image, so a new class can be described in natural language
without retraining~\citep{radford2021clip}. On ImageNet, this zero-shot approach matched
the accuracy of the original ResNet-50 without using any of the 1.28 million training
examples~\citep{radford2021clip}. It therefore suits a project with four material
categories and no labelled marketplace photos, and the project accesses the model through the
Transformers library~\citep{wolf2020transformers}. In prototype testing, CLIP classified
38 of 40 team-collected photos correctly. That sample is small, and a classifier names a
material without describing its condition, so CLIP is used only for classification and a
low-confidence result is flagged for the Admin.

\subsection{Visible Defect Detection}
YOLO frames object detection as a regression problem, in which a single network predicts
bounding boxes and class probabilities from a full image in one
evaluation~\citep{redmon2016yolo}. Compared with other detectors of its time, it made
more localisation errors but fewer false detections on background
regions~\citep{redmon2016yolo}. YOLOv8~\citep{jocher2023yolov8} continues this family and
is the version used in the prototype.

Crack-detection research has moved from fully supervised learning towards
semi-supervised, weakly supervised, few-shot, and domain-adaptation methods, and from
single-dataset results towards cross-dataset evaluation~\citep{crackdetectionreview2025}.
This shift matters because public datasets come from inspection settings:
SDNET2018 contains over 56,000 images of concrete bridge decks, walls, and
pavements~\citep{sdnet2018}, and MBDD2025 contains building surface defects collected by
UAV~\citep{mbdd2025}. In the prototype, YOLOv8n was trained on a public building-surface-defect
dataset~\citep{buildingsurfacedefect} of 5,882 training images and reached a mean average
precision of 0.69 at an IoU threshold of 0.5, with precision 0.70 and recall 0.62, on 735
validation images. The same model produced no detections on a close-up phone photo of rusty
steel, which illustrates the domain gap between inspection images and resale photos. The
project therefore uses material-specific models, measures performance on a small
team-collected set of phone photos, and limits its claims to validated defect classes.

\subsection{Surplus Price Estimation}
Hedonic pricing treats a price as a function of an item's attributes~\citep{rosen1974hedonic},
which matches a lot described by material, quantity, condition, age, and location.
XGBoost is a scalable tree boosting system with a sparsity-aware algorithm for sparse
data~\citep{chen2016xgboost}, which makes it a practical way to learn such a function from
structured attributes. New-material prices are available from published Pakistani
sources~\citep{iconssteelrate,toolsfluentcost2026,thenewscosts2026,zameenprices,graanaprices},
but surplus and resale prices are not published because the market is informal. The model
therefore depends on interviews with dealers and contractors, so its estimate supports a
price check rather than setting a price.

\subsection{Hallucination and Grounding}
Language models can generate content that is not supported by their source, a problem
known as hallucination~\citep{ji2023hallucination}. Restricting a model to supplied
information limits this risk, so the Listing Assistant receives only a lot's structured data
and replies ``not available, contact the seller'' when the data lacks an answer. This limits
hallucination without removing it, so the project measures how often answers are
supported by the lot's data.

The reviewed systems cover discovery and communication well: three of the four document
location search, and the same three document messaging between users. They are weakest on
pre-publication checks. None documents automated material classification, condition
assessment, a description--image consistency check, or human review of flagged listings.
Rheaply comes closest on price, because its AI suggests a market value from an item's
name, but this is a suggestion rather than a fairness check of a seller's price. OLX
Pakistan, Zarea, and Rheaply each include construction-related items among wider catalogues,
so none is specific to construction material. surplusSpace plans to combine these
checks with the location search and chat that users already expect.

\section{Gap Analysis and Proposed Contribution}
This review shows two gaps. The reviewed marketplaces document no check of a listing's
condition, description accuracy, or price fairness before it goes live
(\tref{tab:comparative-analysis}), and public defect datasets target the inspection of
structures rather than the resale of loose material~\citep{sdnet2018,mbdd2025}. surplusSpace
therefore does not propose a new detection or pricing method. Its contribution is to combine
existing methods for material classification, visible-defect detection, condition grading,
description--image consistency checking, and price estimation with Admin review in a
marketplace for four material categories. The contribution is bounded: assessment covers
visible defects only (EXC-02) and applies only to uploaded representative samples (EXC-04),
and this review does not establish that the combination is unique.
